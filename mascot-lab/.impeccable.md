## Design Context

### Users

Project owner comparing tiny mascot candidates locally before choosing a dashboard placement. Task: select characters, compare animated state/personality, inspect legibility at small sizes, and test a dashboard-adjacent preview. This bench is not an emergency response UI.

### Brand Personality

Latest owner feedback: firefighters, a fire chief and drones helping the crew. Mascots must belong to Fire Command Center, not generic robots. Original, friendly, quietly capable. Chief wears a recognizable white fire helmet, front shield, turnout coat, reflective bands and a radio. Ground crew wears distinct fire/rescue gear; drones carry visible camera, relay or support payloads. Small expressive faces, not copied branding or an operational authority.

### Aesthetic Direction

Premium neutral gallery with deliberate 32x32 retro sprites. Shared charcoal/navy turnout gear, white reflective bands, white Chief helmet and restrained red crew helmets (costume detail, not UI accent); blue tech optics link the helping drones to the ground crew. Geist Sans, optional mono metadata, blue #006bff controls, no orange and no emoji. Chief-first dark preview with a real light comparison. Quiet hairlines and clear typography borrow existing Team/FCC and design-demos control language; gear and silhouettes supply identity.

### Design Principles

1. Characters remain legible and distinct at dashboard scale, not just in a hero.
2. Firefighting role must read from the art: helmet, shield, turnout bands, radio, camera/rotors/payload. Not labels pasted onto generic bots.
3. Make local visual-demo status unmistakable; no fictional operational success claims.
4. Every visible control works through mouse and keyboard. Respect reduced motion and explicit pause.
5. Standalone bench only. Dashboard owner chooses whether and where to integrate.

### 2026-11-15 refinement

Chief, Ranger and Rescue form the ground crew. Scout, Relay and Tender are helping drones, with camera, radio or water/support payloads. Chief is the lead mascot, not a model empowered to approve actual missions. Show calm bravery and practical helping, never weaponization, active emergency orders, firefighting efficacy or fake operational status.
